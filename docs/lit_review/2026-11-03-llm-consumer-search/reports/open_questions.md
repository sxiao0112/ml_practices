# Open Questions: Impact of LLMs on consumer search, and how to design or use LLMs for consumer search

These questions remain open after the review, most important first.

| # | Question (plain words) | Why it is still open | What would answer it | Priority |
|---|------------------------|----------------------|----------------------|----------|
| 1 | Do chatbot answers change total spending, or only where people search? | Panels measure search, and purchase tests cover only platform tools | Purchase data linked to a staggered chatbot rollout | High |
| 2 | Do AI assistants raise or lower the prices consumers pay? | Simulations disagree, and no real price data exist | Price records with and without an assistant across many sellers | High |
| 3 | Do labels on sponsored answers change what people buy? | Labels changed attitudes, but no test measured purchases | A preregistered test with purchases as the outcome | High |
| 4 | Do AI shopping picks concentrate sales on a few sellers? | Audits show brand skew, but no sales data link picks to sales | Sales shares before and after assistant use | Medium |
| 5 | Do simulated shoppers predict what individual people choose? | Aggregate fit is good, but individual and subgroup fit is poor | Live tests where simulators and real shoppers face the same choices | Medium |
| 6 | How often do production systems invent product codes? | Papers report accuracy, but not invented-code rates | Logged invented-code rates from a production generative search system | Medium |
| 7 | Do defenses against seller-written text hold against adaptive attacks? | Defenses were tested only on known attacks, with self-reported numbers | An independent test with adaptive attacks and real catalogs | Medium |
| 8 | Do explanations or uncertainty cues help real buyers decide better? | Results conflict across studies and tasks | The same cues tested on the same tasks with purchase outcomes | Medium |
| 9 | Does faster search change long-run choice quality or satisfaction? | Studies run for one session or for 20 weeks at most | A multi-month field study that tracks returns and regret | Low |
| 10 | Do the findings hold outside the U.S. and across age and income groups? | Most samples are U.S. adults recruited online | Cross-country panels with age and income groups | Low |
