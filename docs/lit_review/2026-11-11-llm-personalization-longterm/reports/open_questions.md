# Open Questions: Long-term implications of LLM personalization: feedback loops, dynamic targeting, and how users learn their preferences under LLM influence

These questions remain open after the review, most important first.

| # | Question (plain words) | Why it is still open | What would answer it | Priority |
|---|------------------------|----------------------|----------------------|----------|
| 1 | Do chatbot-driven belief or taste changes last beyond two months? | Only one peer-reviewed study tracks belief change to two months. | A repeated-session trial with real users and a multi-month follow-up. | High |
| 2 | Does personalization add sycophancy beyond repeated use? | Wang 2026 and Akbulut 2026 report opposite results. | Same arms with real users and a multi-month follow-up. | High |
| 3 | Do simulated users drift too little or too much? | Chen 2026 and one follow-up summary point in opposite directions. | Trajectory checks against real panels with matched designs. | High |
| 4 | Does steering by chat ads grow or fade with repeated exposure? | No LLM study runs repeated exposure to chat ads. | A repeated-session field experiment with label conditions. | Medium |
| 5 | Which welfare criterion should a design use? | Stated, reflective and purchase-based welfare can disagree. | Welfare measured after preference shifts on real users. | Medium |
| 6 | Does an advisor's objective change long-run taste? | Formal models assume objectives, and no field test compares them. | A field test comparing engagement-driven and user-goal advisors. | Medium |
| 7 | Does reliance on an AI advisor reduce a person's own taste learning? | Skill loss appears in school tests, but taste learning is untested. | A panel that removes the AI and measures taste over time. | Medium |
| 8 | Does a joint model of a learning user and a strategic LLM predict drift? | Each piece exists alone, and no paper joins them. | A formal model fit to repeated real-user paths. | Low |
